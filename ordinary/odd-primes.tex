\subsection{Character components at odd primes}\label{sec:odd-prime-cases}
\hypertarget{source-ordinary-odd-primes}{}

The remaining odd-prime terms depend on the character through which the abelian group acts. Working in a common cyclotomic field makes it possible to sort these terms simultaneously by character and twist.

Fix the target primitive character \(\psi\) of conductor \(N\)
and order \(o\). At a scale \(x\), write
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal N&=\operatorname{lcm}(2^{e(x)+2},N)\,qd,&
 U&=(\Z/\mathcal N\Z)^\times,\\
 H&=\operatorname{Gal}(\Q(\mu_{\mathcal N})/E),&
 t&=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor .
\end{aligned}
\]
The subgroup \(H\) is defined by the three congruence and character
conditions in Section~\ref{sec:cohomology}. The index \([U:H]\)
divides \(2^{e(x)+2}o\). Indeed, \([U:H]\) is the order of the
image in the product of the real cyclotomic quotient at \(2\),
the image of \(\psi\), and two groups of order two. The two auxiliary
primes \(q,d\) exceed \(2t+1\) and any fixed threshold depending on
\(\psi\) when \(Y_0\) is sufficiently large.

For odd \(\ell\), write \(V^{+}(E,\ell,j)\) and
\(V^{-}(E,\ell,j)\) for the lengths on which complex conjugation,
acting on the localization kernel with the twist included, acts
by \((-1)^j\) and \((-1)^{j+1}\), respectively. The projectors exist
over \(\Z_\ell\), so \(V=V^++V^-\).
For a sufficiently large coefficient ring \(\mathcal O\), let
\[
 \mathcal X_{\ell,j}
  =\{\xi:U/H\longrightarrow\mathcal O^\times:
                     \xi(-1)=(-1)^j\}.
\]

We write \(s_\ell(\xi,j)\) for the normalized length of the
rank-one localization kernel over \(\Q\) with coefficient action
\(\epsilon_\ell^{-j}\xi^{-1}\). Here the local condition is zero
restriction to decomposition groups at primes dividing \(\ell\mathcal N\)
and to inertia groups elsewhere. A length over \(\mathcal O\) is
normalized by dividing by its ramification index over \(\Z_\ell\).
This division removes the change of length under finite coefficient
extension. The action of complex conjugation on
\(\epsilon_\ell^{-j}\xi^{-1}\) for these characters is trivial.

\begin{proposition}[Reduction to rank-one localization kernels]
When
\(\ell\nmid [U:H]\),
\[
 V^+(E,\ell,j)\ \le \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal X_{\ell,j}}s_\ell(\xi,j).
 \tag{P}\label{eq:local-odd-p}
\]
For \(\ell\le\mathcal N\) the integral version gives
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal X_{\ell,j}}s_\ell(\xi,j)
     +O\bigl((1+|\mathcal X_{\ell,j}|)(\mathcal N j)^6\bigr)
 \tag{I}\label{eq:local-odd-i}
\]
as an upper bound on \(V^+(E,\ell,j)\). Here and below an
inequality for lengths includes finiteness when the right
side is finite.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Fourier projection and its integral kernel.}

Put \(G=U/H\) and extend the
coefficients to \(\mathcal O\). For the sign \((-1)^j\),
the maps
\[
                x\longmapsto \sum_{g\in G}\xi(g)^{-1}gx
\]
take values in the \(\xi\)-eigenspaces. Write \(c\in G\) for the conjugation element and
\(s=(-1)^j\). If \(c\ne1\), orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{\xi(c)=s}\xi(g)^{-1}
   = \frac{|G|}{2}\bigl(\mathbf1_{g=1}
                              +s\mathbf1_{g=c}\bigr).
\]
Thus on the sign-\(s\) space, summing the projection maps
gives \(|G|\) times the identity. If \(c=1\), the same
identity holds by summing over all characters for \(s=1\),
and the sign-\((-1)\) space is zero.

Let \(K_0\) be the kernel of the direct sum of the projections.
The preceding identity gives \(|G|K_0=0\), so on its \(\ell\)-primary
coefficients
\[
 \operatorname{length}(K_0)\le v_\ell(|G|)
                  \operatorname{length}(K_0[\ell]).
\]
Indeed the successive \(\ell\)-torsion layers inject into the first by
multiplication by a power of \(\ell\). Use the ambient \(\ell\)-torsion
to bound the last length. The Kummer calculation below gives
dimension \(O(\ell [E:\Q]\log(F\ell))\), with \(F\mid\mathcal N\)
the conductor of \(E\).

\proofstep{Global lifting and the local obstructions.}

For a fixed eigenspace twist the coefficients by \(\xi^{-1}\)
over \(\Q\), so the corresponding classes on \(E\) are
invariant under \(G\). The obstruction to lifting such a class
to \(\Q\) is in \(H^2(G,A^{G_E})\), with \(A\) the divisible
twisted coefficient module. On that eigenspace the global inflation--restriction
sequence has the segment
\[
 H^1(\Q,A)\longrightarrow H^1(E,A)^G
                       \longrightarrow H^2(G,A^{G_E}).
\]
Thus the liftable part has quotient of length at most that of
the degree-two group. Take all lifts of the liftable classes.
At \(p\nmid\ell\mathcal N\) they are unramified since the
inertia group is unchanged in \(E\).

At every
\(p\mid\ell\mathcal N\) the restricted class vanishes in
the decomposition group of \(E_w\). Above \(\ell\) this is
the definition of the kernel. Away from \(\ell\) the class is unramified. On the coefficient
inertia invariants over \(E_w\) the scalar \((Nw)^{-j}-1\) is
nonzero and acts surjectively. Consequently
\[
       H^1_{\rm unr}(E_w,A)=A^{I_w}/(\operatorname{Frob}_w-1)A^{I_w}=0
         \quad (p\ne\ell).
\] The local
obstructions for the lifts over \(\Q\) are therefore in
\(H^1(G_w,A^{G_{E_w}})\) for \(p\mid\ell\mathcal N\),
where \(G_w\le G\) is the decomposition subgroup.
The lifts with all local obstructions zero are precisely
the classes counted by \(s_\ell(\xi,j)\) in this preimage.

\proofstep{Bound the finite coefficient groups.}

At \(p\ne\ell\) the invariant length is bounded by
\[
     v_\ell((Nw)^j-1)\le j[E:\Q]\log p/\log\ell.
\]
Above \(\ell\) the cyclotomic image bounds the length by
\(1+v_\ell([E:\Q])+v_\ell(j)\). The latter also bounds the global
invariant length. The cohomology on finite groups of
order at most \([E:\Q]\) is bounded by the lengths of the
corresponding cochain groups in degrees one and two.
Together with the bound for \(\ell\)-torsion this gives
the error in \eqref{eq:local-odd-i}, using \([E:\Q]\le\mathcal N\) and
\(\ell\le\mathcal N\). If \(\ell\nmid |G|\), the
Fourier kernel vanishes and all the finite-group
obstructions vanish by averaging. This gives \eqref{eq:local-odd-p}.
Alp\"oge's numerical version of \eqref{eq:local-odd-i} bounds the additive error by
\(|\mathcal X_{\ell,j}|(\mathcal Nj)^6\). \label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-1}

\end{proof}

To give the prime and character conditions in the bounds, let
\(p_K,p_1\) be the two fixed primes of the construction for
\(\psi\). The first defines the imaginary quadratic field
\(K_*=\Q(\sqrt{-p_K})\), with discriminant \(-p_K\).
Write \(h_*\) for its class number. The set \(B_\psi\) consists
of \(3\) and the odd prime divisors of \(oNp_Kh_*p_1\).
The reduction gives localization kernels for rank-one characters. Their local and parity conditions now determine which of the trace, deformation and exceptional-prime bounds can be applied.

\begin{lemma}[Prime and character ranges for the odd-prime bounds]
\label{lem:odd-prime-positive-ranges}
Fix \(0<j<t\). For odd \(\ell\notin B_\psi\), the index
\([U:H]\) and all character orders are prime to \(\ell\).
The positive-component bounds at these primes use the following cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\ell-1\nmid2j\), one uses the rank-three trace estimate, a sum over residue
      primes and characters.
\item If \(\ell-1\mid2j\), then \(j\ge2\),
\[
 \ell\le2t-1,
\]
and
      \(\ell\nmid\mathcal N\). The principal term vanishes by Lemma~\ref{lem:principal-odd-strict-zero}. A nonprincipal character of even order
      uses the even-order case and one of odd order uses the rank-four case.
\end{enumerate}
At \(\ell\in B_\psi\), put \(\xi_P\) equal to the prime-to-\(\ell\)
part of \(\xi\) with its \(\ell\)-conductor factor removed.
For \(j\ge2\), the even-order case is used if its order is even and the fixed cyclotomic-layer case
if it is odd. At \(j=1\), the two cases separated by
\[
 R_1(\ell,\xi)\le 2^{c_2(\xi)}
\]
use the case over the Gaussian field and the height
bound for the reverse inequality. Here \(c_2(\xi)\) is the
exponent of 2 in the conductor of the 2-component.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The congruence and character maps defining \(H\) give an injection
\[
 U/H\ \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\
  (\Z/2^{e(x)+2}\Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}
      \ \times\operatorname{im}\psi\times\{\pm1\}^2 .
\]
The right side has order \(2^{e(x)+2}o\) and exponent dividing
\(2^{e(x)+1}o\). Thus for odd \(\ell\notin B_\psi\) both the index
and all character orders are prime to \(\ell\). If \(\ell-1\mid2j\),
then \(j=1\) would force \(\ell=3\), excluded by \(B_\psi\), and
\(\ell\le 2j+1\le2t-1\). In particular \(\ell\) is distinct from
\(q,d>2t+1\). Nor can it divide \(N\), since \(\ell\notin B_\psi\).
Consequently \(\ell\nmid\mathcal N\) and \(\xi_P=\xi\) in the second
case. The principal character calculation is
Lemma~\ref{lem:principal-odd-strict-zero}. The remaining assignments
are the case hypotheses in the indicated length estimates, including
the fixed-prime tests at \(j=1\).
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-2}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summing the rank-three trace bounds}
\label{subsec:odd-prime-trace}

A separate trace bound for each residual prime would accumulate with the number of primes. At fixed twist the rank-three contributions are controlled together by the divisibility of a common trace count.

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous rank-three trace bound]
At each \(0<j<t\), sum the rank-three cases outside \(B_\psi\)
over a finite set of odd residue primes with \(\ell-1\nmid2j\)
and the characters \(\xi\in\mathcal X_{\ell,j}\) assigned to rank three by Lemma~\ref{lem:odd-prime-positive-ranges}. If \(\mathcal I_j\) is the set of these pairs \((\ell,\xi)\), their normalized lengths satisfy 
\[
 \sum_{(\ell,\xi)\in\mathcal I_j}(\log\ell)\,s_\ell(\xi,j)\le(\mathcal N t)^{C_1},
\]
 with \(C_1\) fixed independently of
the auxiliary primes, the scale and the finite set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Group the trace factors by the character kernel.}

The underlying estimate is simultaneous in the residue primes.
Fix \(j\), a subgroup \(J_0\le U\) which is the kernel of a
character, and \(p\ge7\) prime to \(30\mathcal N p_K\).
Fix a place \(w\mid p\) in \(K_*\) and put \(u=Nw\).
There are \(\varphi([U:J_0])\) complex characters with
kernel \(J_0\); these are the faithful characters of the cyclic
quotient \(U/J_0\).

For a character \(\xi\) in this orbit the trace of the reducible
point at inverse Frobenius is
\[
                T^0_\xi=u^{-j}\xi(u)^{-1}+1+u^j\xi(u).
 \tag{T}\label{eq:local-odd-t}
\]
The two packet counts give a finite list of traces \(T_s\)
permuted, with their indices, by field automorphisms. A
degree-three system over \(K_*\) is counted with weights
\((j-1,-1,-j-1)\) after a cyclotomic twist. If its roots
at the chosen place are \(b_i\), the roots for the trace
in this calculation are \((ub_i)^{-1}\).
Their norms are in \([u^{-1/2},u^{1/2}]\) and their
product has norm one by the spectral and determinant bounds.

\proofstep{Spectral bounds on the trace differences.}

For the other count use degree-two systems over \(\Q\)
with weights \((j,-j)\) at level \(\mathcal N |D_{K_*}|\).
For \(u=p^f\), putting \(z_i=b_i^{-f}\) gives
\[
 T_s=1+z_1+z_2,\qquad u^{-1/2}\le |z_i|\le u^{1/2},\qquad
 |z_1||z_2|=1 .
\] In either case
\[
                  |T_s|\le \sqrt u+2.
\]
To see this, set \(x_i=\log|b_i'|\) for the normalized
roots. In rank three \(\sum_i x_i=0\) and
\(-\tfrac12\log u\le x_i\le\tfrac12\log u\).
At a vertex two coordinates are endpoints and their signs are
opposite, so the third is zero.

In rank two the vertices are
the two opposite endpoints. By convexity of the sum of the
exponentials, the maximal sums of root norms are thus
\[
          \sqrt u+1+1/\sqrt u
      \quad\text{and}\quad \sqrt u+1/\sqrt u
\]
respectively. Adding the scalar term in rank two gives the
same bound \(\sqrt u+2\). The separate integrality condition on the inverse roots gives
\(u^{j-1}b_i^{-1}\) integral in degree three and \(p^j b_i^{-1}\)
integral in degree two. It follows that \(u^jT_s\) is integral
in both cases.

For \(u\ge7\), the reducible trace \eqref{eq:local-odd-t} and the packet trace
are distinct, since
\[
 |T^0_\xi|-|T_s|\ge u^j-\sqrt u-3-u^{-j}
                  \ge u/2-3-u^{-1}>0 .
\]
Every factor \(u^j(T_s-T^0_\xi)\) is a nonzero algebraic
integer, of norm at most \(2u^{2j}\). Take their product over the
packet list and over the entire character orbit. Writing \(A\) for this product, every field automorphism permutes its factors. Thus
\[
 A\in\overline{\mathbf Z},\qquad \sigma A=A\ (\sigma\in G_\Q),
 \qquad A\in\overline{\mathbf Z}\cap\Q=\Z .
\] Every factor has \(\ell\)-adic norm at most one by integrality.
Select one point from each grading-torus orbit in the fibre. To bind
its contribution by the packet product, we identify which records can
receive those representatives.
\proofstep{Distinguish the marked packet records.}

The restrictions of these representatives to \(G_{K_*}\)
are pairwise nonisomorphic. Indeed, an intertwiner on
\(G_{K_*}\) commutes with the diagonal marked difference,
whose three eigenvalues are distinct. It is diagonal,
say \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_0,d_1,d_2)\).
The two polarizations imply that
\(D^{-1}JD^{-\mathsf T}J\) commutes with the first
restriction, where \(J\) reverses the three coordinates.
For an irreducible restriction the commutant is scalar. Comparing
the diagonal entries of the commuting matrix gives
\[
 (d_0d_2)^{-1}=d_1^{-2} .
\]

For an outer plane plus a middle line, change the middle scalar
to a square root of \(d_0d_2\) without changing the intertwining.
With the resulting choice \(d_1^2=d_0d_2\), put \(s=d_0/d_1\). The scalar-normalized intertwiner is
\[
 d_1^{-1}D=\operatorname{diag}(s,1,d_2/d_1)
           =\operatorname{diag}(s,1,s^{-1}).
\]
The marked point maps are determined by their matrix
entries on the quadratic kernel. Their matrices on the
other coset follow from the fixed value at complex
conjugation. Hence an isomorphism of restrictions gives
a grading-torus equivalence of the point maps.

The chosen representatives inject into the packet indices,
since a complete system determines the isomorphism class
of its attached semisimple restriction. The corresponding
factor has norm equal to that of its trace difference since
\(u\) is an \(\ell\)-adic unit. The whole product is a nonzero
integer \(A_{J_0}(p)\), independent of \(\ell\). The product of
the \(\ell\)-adic norms of the selected trace differences
in all fibers is at least \(|A_{J_0}(p)|_\ell\). Its size satisfies
\[
 \log |A_{J_0}(p)|
    \ll_{\psi}\varphi([U:J_0])\bigl(\mathcal N(j+1)\bigr)^a
         \log p
 \tag{D}\label{eq:local-odd-d}
\]
with a fixed exponent \(a\). This uses \(\log u\le2\log p\)
and the packet counts polynomial in \(\mathcal N(j+1)\)
and \(\mathcal N |D_{K_*}|(j+1)\), respectively. The
construction and admissibility of those packets are the inputs
to this product calculation.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-3}

Here is the valuation argument once the admitted points have been
constructed. For \(\ell\ne p\), fix a coefficient embedding into
\(\C\). Existence of one coefficient-field character of kernel
\(J_0\) supplies all the faithful characters of \(U/J_0\) by
taking powers. Its embedding identifies this orbit bijectively
with the complex orbit in the packet bound, since an embedding
preserves the kernel. The one-character deformation estimate
gives a finite set \(X_\xi\) of admitted points, distinct up
to isomorphism in each fiber, with
\[
 s_\ell(\xi,j)\ \le\
 2\sum_{\rho\in X_\xi}
 v_\ell\!\left(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{w}^{-1})
                 -(u^{-j}\xi(u)^{-1}+1+u^j\xi(u))\right).
 \tag{L}\label{eq:local-odd-l}
\]
\proofstep{Apply the one-character deformation inequality.}

The nonvanishing for each point follows by applying the packet
bound to a singleton. Since the character order is prime to
\(\ell\), one can carry out the deformation calculation over the
unramified ring containing its values. Scalar extension to
the chosen coefficient ring preserves the normalized lengths
and valuations. The residual hypotheses of that calculation
hold for these hosts: \(\mathcal N\) is even,
\(\ell\nmid D_{K_*}h_*\operatorname{ord}(\xi)\),
the \(\ell\)-component of \(\xi\) has order dividing two, and
the residual quadratic character of \(K_*\) is nontrivial on the
intersection of the kernels of the residual cyclotomic character
and the character \(\xi_P\). For the last assertion use the ramified place \(p_K\nmid\ell\mathcal N\).
On its inertia group one has
\[
 \bar\epsilon_\ell=1,\qquad\bar\xi_P=1,\qquad
            \bar\chi_{K_*}\ne1
\]
as characters. This is the required kernel test. These are the field conditions for \eqref{eq:local-odd-l}, whose other inputs are deformation theory and automorphy. \label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-4}

\proofstep{Use the same nonzero integers across residue primes.}

The trace comparison gives divisibility by powers of each residual prime. To sum their logarithms, these powers must divide the same nonzero integers with size bounded independently of that prime.


Applying the comparison to all the \(X_\xi\) at once gives
\(\sum_{\ker\xi=J_0}s_\ell(\xi,j)\le2v_\ell(A_{J_0}(p))\).
Choose two distinct test primes above a threshold depending only
on \(\psi\), and require \(q,d\) to exceed both. They avoid
\(30\mathcal N p_K\) since the only prime factors of \(\mathcal N\)
are those of \(2Nqd\). Use the second prime if \(\ell\) equals
the first.

For every finite residue-prime set the weighted
sum for \(J_0\) is at most
\[
 2\log|A_{J_0}(p)|+2\log|A_{J_0}(p')|,
\]
by prime factorization of the two integers. Only kernels realized
in the chosen character sets need be included. The sum of
\(\varphi([U:J_0])\) over these kernels is at most \(|U|\),
since each character of \(U\) has a unique kernel. Summing \eqref{eq:local-odd-d}
therefore proves the bound \((\mathcal Nt)^{C_1}\) in the rank-three trace estimate
for each \(j\), with \(C_1\) fixed before the auxiliary primes and
\(x\) are chosen.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-5}

\end{proof}

\subsection{Pointwise bounds for the other positive components}
\label{subsec:odd-prime-pointwise}

The rank-three trace sum already treats the main range of positive components. For the other cases the deformation estimate and the bounds at fixed exceptional primes must be matched to the original character decomposition.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise and fixed-layer bounds in the remaining cases]\label{prop:other-positive-cohomology}
Use the cases of Lemma~\ref{lem:odd-prime-positive-ranges}.
The two nonprincipal cases covered by the rank-four deformation
theory have 
\[
 \log\ell\,s_\ell(\xi,j)\le(\ell\mathcal Nj)^C.
\]

At the fixed exceptional primes use the Gaussian bound or the
height estimate in their respective \(j=1\) ranges, and use the
fixed-layer estimate \textup{\eqref{eq:local-odd-w}} for the corresponding odd-order
case with conductor dividing \(N\). The constants in these bounds
precede variation of the selected auxiliary primes and scale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{The even-order and rank-four ranges.}

The inputs for these bounds are the length estimate
\eqref{eq:rankfour-length} and the packet conditions of
Propositions~\ref{prop:count-two-quadratic-packets}
and \ref{prop:count-three-host-output}.
For the two cases distinguished by the parity of the character order,
the data of Section~\ref{sec:rank-four-deformation}
are built after adjoining \(2^{n_{\rm II}}\)-th and
\(2^{n_{\rm III}}\)-th roots of unity to the coefficient field.
Let \(m\) be the least common multiple of \(4\) and the order
of the prime-to-\(\ell\) part of the odd-conductor factor of
\(\xi\). Put \(f=\operatorname{ord}_{(\Z/m\Z)^\times}(\ell)\)
and \(n_0=v_2(\ell^f-1)\).

In terms of these numbers the two
exponents used are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary-dyadic-exponents}
\begin{split}
 n_{\rm II}&=\left\lceil\log_2\max\{\ell\mathcal N4^{n_0},
             2^{c_2(\xi)+6}o\ell^3N^2\}\right\rceil,\\
 n_{\rm III}&=\left\lceil\log_2\max\{\ell Np_14^{n_0},
             8\ell^2\}\right\rceil .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The corresponding rational levels are \(M=\ell\,2^n\mathcal N b\),
where \((n,b)=(n_{\rm II},1)\) or \((n_{\rm III},p_1)\).
The odd-conductor factor of \(\xi\) has order dividing \(2o\);
thus \(m\mid4o\) and \(f\le4o\). For odd \(\ell\), the
factorization of \(\ell^f-1\) gives
\[
 2^{v_2(\ell^f-1)}\le 2^{v_2(f)}(\ell^2-1).
\]
For odd \(f\) the quotient by \(\ell-1\) is odd.
For even \(f\) write \(f=2^af_0\) with \(f_0\) odd. Factoring
successive squares gives
\[
 v_2(\ell^f-1)=v_2(\ell-1)+v_2(\ell+1)+a-1.
\]
The additional factors after \(\ell+1\) are \(2\bmod8\).
In both cases \(f\le4o\) gives
\[
 4^{n_0}\le(4o)^2\ell^4.
\]

Since
\(2^{c_2(\xi)}\le\mathcal N\) and the ceiling in the
definition of \(n\) changes \(2^n\) by a factor less than two,
each term inside the maximum is bounded by a fixed
power of \(\ell\mathcal N\). The same holds for
\(M=\ell\,2^n\mathcal N b\), as \(b=1\) or \(p_1\) is fixed. The same
holds for \(k=(\ell-1)2^n(2o)\), which bounds the orders of the
roots of unity in the reducible trace. Injectivity of the
coefficient embedding preserves kernels, local orders,
conductors and parity. For a finite extension of coefficient DVRs \(O'/O\) of ramification index \(e'\), each Smith cyclic summand \(O/(\varpi^a)\) acquires length \(e'a\). Hence for the finite module \(M_0\)
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{length}_{O'}(M_0\otimes_O O')}{e(O'/\Z_\ell)}
 =\frac{e'\operatorname{length}_O(M_0)}{e' e(O/\Z_\ell)}
 =\frac{\operatorname{length}_O(M_0)}{e(O/\Z_\ell)} .
\]

Select a prime \(p\ge5\) not dividing \(\ell\mathcal N b\).
One can take \(p\le 6\ell\mathcal N b+1\) by taking a prime
divisor of \(6\ell\mathcal N b+1\). Such a prime is also
prime to \(2^n\). The reducible arithmetic Frobenius trace is
\[
                    p^{j+1}z_1+p^jz_2+pz_3+1,\qquad z_i^k=1 .
\]
In the even-order and odd-order cases the admitted points are the irreducible
points on their respective rational charts. In the even-order case,
Paragraphs~\ref{par:count-two-descent} and~\ref{par:rigid-point}
supply the irreducible quadratic restrictions at level \(M\)
and the tangent condition. The corresponding Inputs for the rank-four construction
are in Section~\ref{sec:count-three-target}. The ordinary
construction gives the degree-zero finiteness and block
exclusion on each chart. Thus \eqref{eq:rankfour-length} applies
once the competing traces are separated from the reducible trace.

\proofstep{Nonvanishing and integrality of the trace differences.}

For a competing packet, let \(P=p^{(j+1)/2}\).
Write \(z'_1,\ldots,z'_4\) for its roots. The symplectic
determinant and the local unitary bound give
\[
          \prod_i|z'_i|=P^4,\qquad \max_i|z'_i|\le\sqrt p\,P .
\]
At the chosen place of a quadratic restriction of residue
degree \(f\), the determinant of the symplectic representation
is the square of its similitude. The latter is a finite
character times \(\epsilon_\ell^{j+1}\), giving product
of norms \(p^{2f(j+1)}\). The cuspidal local representation
is generic and essentially unitary. At this spherical place
the generic unitary classification bounds each exponent
after the common unitary normalization by \(1/2\) in
absolute value. Taking \(f\)-th roots gives the asserted
bound at the rational prime. The eigensystem count retains
these norm conditions at every complex conjugate of the
normalized parameters, since conjugation preserves the
algebraic weight and conjugates the finite characters.

If \(T\) denotes the competing trace, at least one root
has norm at most \(P\), since the four norms multiply to
\(P^4\). Using \(j\ge2\) and \(p\ge5\), we get
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |T-p^jz_2-pz_3-1|
    &\le 3\sqrt p\,P+P+p^j+p+1\\
    &<3p^j+\tfrac12p^j+p^j+\tfrac12p^j=5p^j.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sqrt p\,P\le p^j\), \(2P\le p^j\) and
\(p+1<p^j/2\). The remaining term has norm \(p^{j+1}\ge5p^j\). Thus the
trace difference \(D=T-(p^{j+1}z_1+p^jz_2+pz_3+1)\) satisfies
\[
        0<p^{j+1}-|T-p^jz_2-pz_3-1|\le|D|<2p^{j+1}.
\]
For integrality of the inverse geometric Frobenius roots,
use the Galois attachments of the regular algebraic polarized
cuspidal eigensystem at every coefficient prime. At a
coefficient prime away from \(p\) the roots are units,
by compactness of the Galois image.

At a coefficient prime
above \(p\), use local--global compatibility for the unramified
parameter. The representation there is potentially crystalline:
its Weil--Deligne monodromy is bounded by the zero monodromy
of the spherical parameter. The dual crystalline filtered
module after finite extension has filtration jumps \(0,1,j,j+1\)
at each embedding. If \(D_0\) is the smallest-slope submodule, its induced filtration has only nonnegative jumps. Thus
\[
 t_N(D_0)\ge t_H(D_0)\ge0 .
\]
The first Newton slope is nonnegative, and all remaining slopes are at least as large. Its Frobenius
eigenvalues are powers of the inverse geometric Frobenius
roots in the normalization under consideration. Thus for a rational packet root \(\alpha\) whose \(f\)-th power occurs in this list,
\[
 v_p(\alpha)=f^{-1}v_p(\alpha^f)\ge0.
\]

\hfill\mbox{\cite{tadic,blggt,blggt-lg,colmez-fontaine}}\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-6}

Multiply these trace differences over the packet records and
over the action of \((\Z/k\Z)^\times\) on the \(z_i\), retaining
the record and the action index even when numerical values
coincide. The records consist of a quadratic field, a full
cuspidal eigensystem, an inverse-image multiset of its roots
at the test prime and one of two copies for the rational
descents. By the packet comparison, distinct isomorphism
classes admit distinct records. Field automorphisms permute
the eigensystems, the inverse-image multisets and the actions
on the \(z_i\), keeping the two copies. Thus the product \(D_p\)
is a nonzero rational integer.

If there are at most
\(c(M(j+1))^a\) records, then
\[
 \log|D_p|\le c\varphi(k)(M(j+1))^a
                  \bigl(\log 2+(j+1)\log p\bigr).
\]
Each point of \eqref{eq:rankfour-length} contributes a distinct
factor in the identity slice of \((\Z/k\Z)^\times\).
All factors have nonnegative \(\ell\)-adic valuation by
integrality. Dividing the length comparison by the coefficient
ramification index gives
\[
 \log\ell\,s_\ell(\xi,j)
   \ \le\ 2v_\ell(D_p)\log\ell
   \ \le\ 2\log|D_p|
   \ \le\ (\ell\mathcal N j)^C .
\]
The constants depend on the fixed primitive target and
the fixed quadratic list. The size estimates are uniform in
\(\ell,\xi,j\) and the conductor of the auxiliary field, since
\(p\le6\ell\mathcal N b+1\), the factor \(b\) is fixed
in each case, and \(k,M\) are bounded by fixed powers of
\(\ell\mathcal N\). The fitted fields, their exceptional
places and the coefficient extensions do not enter the
bound. This proves the positive-component estimates in the
even-order and rank-four cases on the given fields.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-7}

\proofstep{The Gaussian case and complementary height bound.}

For the Gaussian case, take \(K=\Q(i)\) and \(j=1\).
The two-power conductor satisfies
\[
 2^{c_2(\xi)}\ge R_1(\ell,\xi)
       =16o(\ell N)^2\,4^{n_0}.
\]
At an inert prime \(p\) of \(K\) the residue size is \(u=p^2\).
At the reducible point the trace is the value of \eqref{eq:local-odd-t} there:
\[
          T^0_\xi=p^{-2}\xi(p^2)^{-1}+1+p^2\xi(p^2).
\]
Such a prime can be chosen with \(7\le p\le12\ell\mathcal N\)
and \(p\nmid\ell\mathcal N\). Take a prime divisor congruent
to \(3\pmod4\) of \(12\ell\mathcal N-1\), which is coprime
to \(3\ell\mathcal N\).
In the rank-three packet product use the entire
\(\varphi(\mathcal N)\)-th root group in place of the orbit
of one character. This includes \(\xi(u)\) and is permuted
by every field automorphism. Restrict to \(G_K\) in the
packet injection.

The image of \(G_K\) in the host quotient
is the kernel of its quadratic character. Thus points
distinct on that subgroup remain distinct on restriction
to \(G_K\). If automorphy is given with different
embeddings of \(K\), partition the points by the two
embeddings before applying the product comparison. The
sum of their valuations times \(\log\ell\) is bounded by
\[
      O\!\left(2\varphi(\mathcal N)
                  (\mathcal N(j+1))^a\log p\right).
\]
The abstract deformation count gives a length at most
twice this sum of valuations times the coefficient
ramification index, and bounds the rank-one kernel by
that length. Dividing by the ramification index cancels that coefficient price.
Together with \(p\le12\ell\mathcal N\) the product bound gives
\[
       (\log\ell)s_\ell(\xi,1)\le(\ell\mathcal N)^C
\]
for fixed \(C\). The two embeddings contribute a
constant because the same Gaussian field is used at every
scale. The inputs here are the reduced unitary lift and rigidity on its field and frame. \label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-8}

For \(j=1\) and \(2^{c_2(\xi)}< R_1(\ell,\xi)\),
one uses the height estimate instead. Here \(\ell\) belongs
to the fixed set \(B_\psi\). The odd-conductor character
factor has order dividing \(2o\) and the 2-component
has order at most \(2^{c_2(\xi)}\), so \(\operatorname{ord}(\xi)\)
is bounded in terms of \(\psi\). The field \(L\) cut out
by \(\xi\) and \(\mu_\ell\) has degree at most a constant
\(n_\psi\) and
\[
 \log |D_L|\le n_\psi\log(\ell\mathcal N)
\]

by the conductor--discriminant formula. Restrict the
rank-one kernel to \(L\) to remove the character.
Inflation--restriction bounds the kernel of this restriction
by degree-one cohomology on the finite Galois group
with invariant coefficients. Writing \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\Q)\) and \(A\) for its invariant coefficient module in inflation--restriction gives
\[
 |H^1(G,A)|\le |Z^1(G,A)|\le |A|^{|G|},\qquad |G|=[L:\Q] .
\]
The second bound counts all functions \(G\to A\). Local invariant
orders in this calculation have normalized length at most
\(2\mathcal N j\) at twist \(j\), and the same bound
applies to the global invariants by restriction to one
decomposition group. At \(j=1\) the finite-group lifting cost is bounded by
\[
            [L:\Q](2\mathcal N)\le2n_\psi\mathcal N.
\] The height estimate
of Section~\ref{sec:cohomology} for the dual kernel on \(L\),
with \(j=1\) and bounded degree, is polynomial in
\(\ell\mathcal N\). This proves the required pointwise
bound in the small-conductor branch.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-9}

\proofstep{Sum first over twists on the fixed layer.}

The exceptional-prime bounds use one reference layer for several twists. Summing on that fixed layer first keeps the cost of the common window from being repeated.


Finally, in the fixed cyclotomic-layer case, odd order of \(\xi_P\) forces the
conductor of \(\xi\) to divide \(N\). Its 2-component is
trivial. For \(v\in\{q,d\}\) the defining subgroup gives a local factor of order dividing two. Its prime-to-\(\ell\) part is a factor of the odd-order \(\xi_P\), so
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\xi_v)\mid\gcd(2,\operatorname{ord}(\xi_P))=1 .
\]
Here \(\ell\) is odd, so passage to the prime-to-\(\ell\) part preserves any sign.
Removing the \(\ell\)-power part of the character
cannot affect their 2-primary components.
As \(\ell\ne q,d\), this argument is independent of the
\(\ell\)-conductor part.

Choose the reference layer \(L_\ell\)
for the cyclotomic \(\Z_\ell\)-extension of
\(\Q(\mu_{\ell N})\). Its depth is fixed by the tower rank
bound, before choosing \(q,d,x\). It contains the field of
every character of conductor dividing \(N\). At each \(1\le j<t\), restriction to \(L_\ell\) has
finite-group kernel of normalized length at most
\(2[L_\ell:\mathbb Q]\mathcal N j\) by inflation--restriction.
Summing gives
\[
         \sum_{1\le j<t}s_\ell(\xi,j)\le B+(\mathcal Nt)^C ,
         \qquad B=\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5}),
 \tag{W}\label{eq:local-odd-w}
\]
where \(B\) bounds the sum of the kernels over \(L_\ell\)
on this window. Summing the finite-group restriction loss gives
\[
        2[L_\ell:\Q]\mathcal N \sum_{1\le j<t}j
             =[L_\ell:\Q]\mathcal N t(t-1).
\] The degree \([L_\ell:\mathbb Q]\) depends only on \(\psi,\ell\), and the estimate \eqref{eq:local-odd-w}
is uniform in the host and in \(\xi\). Every kernel in
\eqref{eq:local-odd-w} is finite by the same restriction.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-10}

\end{proof}

\subsection{Summation over a finite set of odd primes}
\label{subsec:odd-prime-sum}

The bounds so far apply to different sets of characters and primes. When they are added, the exceptional contribution from the common window must be counted only once, independently of the finite prime set.

\begin{proposition}[Total positive length on a finite prime set]
On the chosen window, for each finite set \(S\) of odd primes the positive sum in \textup{\eqref{eq:local-odd-splus}} satisfies 
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in S}(\log\ell)\sum_{1\le j<t}V^+(E,\ell,j)\le A^C+CB,
\]
 where \(A=\mathcal Nt\), \(B=\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\), and \(C\)
is independent of \(S,x,q,d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Primes outside the fixed exceptional set.}

Put \(A=\mathcal Nt\) and let \(S\) be any finite set of odd
primes. For \(\ell\notin B_\psi\), apply \eqref{eq:local-odd-p}.
At each \(j\), the rank-three trace estimate contributes at most \(A^{C_1}\).
The other cases have \(\ell\le2t\) and
\(\ell\mathcal Nj\le A^3\). There are at most \(2t\) such primes and at most
\(|U|\le\mathcal N\) characters per prime. Their pointwise
bounds contribute at most
\[
       t(2t)\mathcal N(A^3)^C
\]
to the sum over \(j\), where \(C\) covers the two nonprincipal
cases. The principal terms vanish.
Singleton prime sets give finiteness for all the component
lengths used in this sum.

\proofstep{Fixed exceptional primes and the single tower allowance.}

At fixed primes one tower contribution covers all twists.


For each \(\ell\in B_\psi\), apply \eqref{eq:local-odd-i}. The error summed
over all characters and twists is
\(O(\mathcal N t A^6\log\ell)\). The remaining polynomial terms
from the even-order case, the Gaussian case and the height
estimate are at most a fixed
power of \(A\), since now \(\ell<q\le\mathcal N\) and hence
\(\ell\mathcal Nj\le A^2\). In the tower case sum over all
twists for each character first. The characters subject to \eqref{eq:local-odd-w}
have conductor dividing \(N\), so at most \(\varphi(N)\)
distinct characters occur in this sum even as the twist
changes. After including the weight \(\log\ell\) the tower term is at most
\[
        B\,\varphi(N)\sum_{\ell\in B_\psi}\log\ell .
\]
The coefficient of \(B\) is fixed by the target character. In particular,
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in S}\log\ell\sum_{1\le j<t}V^+(E,\ell,j)
       \le A^C+C B
 \tag{S+}\label{eq:local-odd-splus}
\]
for a constant \(C\) independent of \(S,x,q,d\).
The order of summation in the tower term is needed to
retain the single allowance from the common window.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-11}

\end{proof}

\subsection{Negative components and the joint bound}
\label{subsec:odd-prime-negative}

The positive-character sum still omits the negative components and the prime two. A polynomial bound for the negative sum leaves enough room for the dyadic contribution on the same interval.

\begin{proposition}[Negative lengths and the joint bound on the window]\label{prop:cohomology-joint-window}
For the chosen field and interval, there exists a fixed \(C\) such that every finite set \(S\) of odd primes satisfies 
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in S}(\log\ell)\sum_{1\le j<t}V^-(E,\ell,j)\le(\mathcal N t)^C.
\]
 Together with the positive sum and
Proposition~\ref{prop:dyadic-window-total}, the joint finite-set
bound is \eqref{eq:cohomwin}, with the same field and the strict
range \(1\le j<t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Critical-parity Bernoulli numbers.}

The negative components use the critical parity
\(\xi(-1)=(-1)^{j+1}\), with Bernoulli index \(k=j+1\).
Write \(F\) for the conductor of \(E\) and \(n_E=[E:\Q]\).
For a Dirichlet character \(\eta\) of modulus \(F\) let
\[
    a_{F,k}(\eta)=F\!\prod_{p\le k+1}p\ B_{k,\eta^\circ},
\]
where \(\eta^\circ\) is its primitive character and
\(B_{k,\eta^\circ}\) denotes the generalized Bernoulli number.
The addition formula for Bernoulli polynomials and the
von Staudt--Clausen denominators show that \(a_{F,k}(\eta)\)
is an algebraic integer. In fact, if \(f\mid F\) is the
conductor of \(\eta^\circ\), the coefficient of \(\eta^\circ(a)\)
in \(B_{k,\eta^\circ}\) is
\[
   f^{k-1}B_k(a/f)=\sum_{r=0}^k\binom{k}{r}
                      B_r\,a^{k-r}f^{r-1}.
\]
Its denominators divide \(F\prod_{p\le k+1}p\).

The functional equation relates \(B_{k,\eta^\circ}\) in the
stated parity to the nonzero value of the conjugate
\(L\)-function at \(k\ge2\), so this number is nonzero.
The Fourier expansion gives
\(\sup_{[0,1]}|B_k|\le2k!\zeta(k)/(2\pi)^k\).
Since \(\prod_{p\le h}p\le4^h\), this gives
\[
       |a_{F,k}(\eta)|\le16 F^{k+1}k! .
 \tag{B}\label{eq:local-odd-b}
\]
For \(P(x)=\prod_{p\le x}p\), the primes in the upper interval
divide the corresponding binomial coefficient, so
\[
      P(2h)\le P(h)\binom{2h}{h},\qquad
      P(2h+1)\le P(h+1)\binom{2h+1}{h}.
\]
The even central binomial coefficient is bounded by its row sum \(2^{2h}\). For the odd row, the two central entries agree, giving
\[
 2\binom{2h+1}{h}\le\sum_{i=0}^{2h+1}\binom{2h+1}{i}=2^{2h+1}.
\]
Both central coefficients used in the induction are therefore at most \(2^{2h}\). This
proves \(P(x)\le4^x\) by induction.

We give the negative-component estimate using Greither and
Popescu's theorem on the higher Stickelberger ideal. The primitive-character bound uses reciprocity. The annihilator uses an integral smoothed Stickelberger element. The published Fitting ideal
formula gives the two estimates needed here from the same input.
\hfill\mbox{\cite{greither-popescu}}\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-12}

\proofstep{Fitting ideals on the critical sign.}

Let \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\Q)\), and let \(c\in G\) be complex
conjugation. For every odd \(\ell\), let \(S_\ell\) consist of the
archimedean place and the primes dividing \(\ell F\).
Write
\[
 M_\ell^i=H^i_{\rm et}(\mathcal O_{E,S_\ell},\Z_\ell(k)),
\]

where the primes inverted in \(E\) lie over \(S_\ell\).
Poitou--Tate identifies the original localization kernel with
the dual of a submodule of \(H^2_{\rm et}(\mathcal O_E[1/\ell],
\Z_\ell(k))\). The map from this \(H^2\) to \(M_\ell^2\)
is injective. For each newly inverted prime with residue field \(\kappa\), its
local \(H^0\) is the kernel of
\[
      (|\kappa|^{k-1}-1):\Z_\ell(k-1)\longrightarrow \Z_\ell(k-1).
\]
This vanishes for \(k\ge2\), giving injectivity in the localization
sequence.
All maps commute with \(G\). In particular, for
\(e_k=(1+(-1)^k c)/2\), the length \(V^-(E,\ell,j)\) is
bounded by the length of \(e_k M_\ell^2\).

Theorem~6.11 of Greither and Popescu applies to the abelian
CM extension \(E/\Q\), the set \(S_\ell\) containing the
ramified and \(\ell\)-adic places, and \(k\ge2\).
The required cyclotomic \(\mu\)-invariant for \(E(\mu_\ell)\)
vanishes by Ferrero--Washington because that field is abelian
over \(\Q\). Put \(\Theta_\ell=\Theta_{S_\ell,E/\Q}(1-k)\).
The theorem states
\[
 e_k\,\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Z_\ell[G]}(M_\ell^2)
   = \operatorname{Ann}_{\Z_\ell[G]}((M_\ell^1)_{\rm tors})
       \,\Theta_\ell .
 \tag{F}\label{eq:local-odd-f}
\]
The evaluation of \(\Theta_\ell\) at a character of \(G\) is
the corresponding \(S_\ell\)-incomplete \(L\)-value at \(1-k\),
with inversion of the character in the group-ring convention.

We index the evaluations by that inverted character \(\eta\).
This preserves the parity and the full character set. Up to
sign this evaluation is
\[
 \frac{B_{k,\eta^\circ}}{k}\,
     \prod_{p\mid F\ell}(1-\eta^\circ(p)p^{k-1}).
\]
A missing Euler factor at a ramified place is interpreted as
one. When \(\ell\nmid F\), the only factor in the displayed
product which depends on \(\ell\) is an \(\ell\)-adic unit.
\hfill\mbox{\cite{greither-popescu}}

\proofstep{The cyclotomic degree-one term.}

The torsion in \(M_\ell^1\) is \(H^0(E,\Q_\ell/\Z_\ell(k))\),
by the coefficient exact sequence and the vanishing of
\(H^0(E,\Q_\ell(k))\). Let \(\ell^{r_\ell}\) kill this group.
The cyclotomic image of \(G_E\) has index dividing \(n_E\)
in \(\Z_\ell^\times\). Its intersection with
\(1+\ell\Z_\ell\) contains \(1+\ell^{1+v_\ell(n_E)}\Z_\ell\).

For a generator of that subgroup, the valuation of its \(k\)-th
power minus one is \(1+v_\ell(n_E)+v_\ell(k)\), so we can take
\[
                 r_\ell=1+v_\ell(n_E)+v_\ell(k).
\]
If \(\ell\nmid F\) and \(\ell-1\nmid k\), the whole cyclotomic
image on \(\mu_\ell\) is realized over \(E\). Indeed every
nontrivial subfield of \(\Q(\mu_\ell)\) is ramified at
\(\ell\), whereas \(E\) is unramified there. A generator of the
residual image then acts with \(k\)-th power different from
one, and we can use \(r_\ell=0\).

Define the nonzero integer
\[
 J_{F,k}=
   \prod_{\substack{\eta\in\widehat G\\ \eta(c)=(-1)^k}}
    \left(a_{F,k}(\eta)
                 \prod_{p\mid F}(1-\eta^\circ(p)p^{k-1})\right).
\]
The factors are algebraic integers permuted by field
automorphisms. All the Euler factors are nonzero since
\(k-1>0\). The use of \(\eta^\circ\) makes the Euler factors
correct even when \(\eta\) is imprimitive at level \(F\).
The bound \eqref{eq:local-odd-b} and
\(\prod_{p\mid F}(1+p^{k-1})\le 2^{\omega(F)}F^{k-1}\le F^k\)
give
\[
                   \log |J_{F,k}|\ll n_E k\log(Fk).
\]

\proofstep{Integral lengths in the prime-to-degree case.}

For \(\ell\nmid n_E\), extend \(\Z_\ell\) to a ring of
integers containing all \(G\)-character values. Its integral idempotents are
\[
     e_\eta=\frac1{n_E}\sum_{g\in G}\eta(g)^{-1}g
          \quad(\eta\in\widehat G).
\]
They split its group ring as a product of coefficient rings. The Fitting ideal of a finite module
over a discrete valuation ring has valuation equal to its length,
by the elementary divisor theorem. Thus \eqref{eq:local-odd-f} bounds the normalized
length of the \(\eta\) component by the valuation of
\(\ell^{r_\ell}\eta^{-1}(\Theta_\ell)\).
Here \(r_\ell-v_\ell(k)\le 1\). The allowance of one is
needed only when \(\ell\mid F\) or \(\ell-1\mid k\); the latter
implies \(\ell\le k+1\).

In both cases the denominator-clearing
factor \(F\prod_{p\le k+1}p\) supplies it. The sum of the
component lengths is therefore at most \(v_\ell(J_{F,k})\).
Consequently for every finite set of odd primes prime to \(n_E\),
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in S,\ \ell\nmid n_E}\log\ell\ V^-(E,\ell,j)
       \le \log |J_{F,k}|\ll n_E k\log(Fk).
\]
With \(k=j+1\le t\), summation uses
\[
 \sum_{1\le j<t}n_E(j+1)\log(F(j+1))\ll n_Et^2\log(Ft).
\]

If an odd \(\ell\) divides \(n_E\), it divides \(o\) by
the bound for the index of the Galois subgroup. Work on the lattice \(e_k\Z_\ell[G]\).
\proofstep{The degree-dividing primes.}

The element \(\ell^{r_\ell}\Theta_\ell\) is integral by
\eqref{eq:local-odd-f}, belongs to the Fitting ideal on this sign, and hence
annihilates \(e_k M_\ell^2\). Multiplication by the element
has nonzero determinant on the lattice. If \(T\) is its multiplication operator on \(e_k\Z_\ell[G]\),
Cayley--Hamilton gives its adjugate as a polynomial in \(T\).
Then
\[
        T\operatorname{adj}(T)=\det(T)\,I
\]
implies that the determinant scalar annihilates the sign module.
Its valuation is the sum over the sign characters of the
valuations of \(\ell^{r_\ell}\eta^{-1}(\Theta_\ell)\).
Using the above denominator clearing, it is at most
\(n_E r_\ell+v_\ell(J_{F,k})\).
Thus the \(\ell\)-power annihilator on the kernel can be
chosen with exponent \(r\) satisfying
\[
   r\log\ell\ll_\psi n_E(j+1)\log(j\ell F).
 \tag{K}\label{eq:local-odd-k}
\]
The constant is uniform since \(v_\ell(n_E)\le v_\ell(o)\)
at odd \(\ell\) and \(k=j+1\).
\hfill\mbox{\cite{greither-popescu}}

\proofstep{The Kummer generator bound.}

For completeness, the number of generators needed with
\eqref{eq:local-odd-k} can be bounded directly. Let \(L=E(\mu_\ell)\), of
degree \(n_L\le\ell n_E\). The \(\ell\)-torsion of the
global \(H^1\) with coefficient \(\Q_\ell/\Z_\ell(-j)\)
is a quotient of the corresponding mod-\(\ell\) \(H^1\).
On the mod-\(\ell\) cohomology the identity
\[
 \operatorname{cor}_{L/E}\operatorname{res}_{L/E}=[L:E]\,\mathrm{id}
\]
exhibits a left inverse to restriction after division by the prime-to-\(\ell\) degree. Over \(L\) the residual
action is trivial, and Kummer theory bounds its dimension
by \(n_L+|S_L|+\log h_L/\log\ell\), where \(S_L\) is the
set of primes over \(\ell\) and \(h_L\) is the ordinary
ideal class number. Each ideal class has a representative of norm at most
\(\sqrt{|D_L|}\) by Minkowski. Weighting its count by the
square of the norm bound gives
\[
 h_L\le |D_L|\sum_{\mathfrak a\ne0}
                         (\operatorname N\mathfrak a)^{-2}
       \le |D_L|\zeta(2)^{n_L} .
\]

The abelian discriminant estimate is \(\log |D_L|\le n_L\log(F\ell)\).
With \(|S_L|\le n_L\) and \(n_L=[L:E]n_E\le(\ell-1)n_E\),
the mod-\(\ell\) generator bound becomes
\[
 n_L+|S_L|+\frac{\log h_L}{\log\ell}
     \le 2n_L+
             \frac{n_L(\log(F\ell)+\log\zeta(2))}{\log\ell}
     \ll \ell n_E\log(F\ell).
\]
The implied constant is independent of \(j\) in this case.
The \(\ell^r\)-torsion has length at most \(r\) times
the length of the \(\ell\)-torsion. This follows from
the filtration by the powers of \(\ell\), since multiplication
by \(\ell^{i-1}\) injects the \(i\)-th quotient of the
torsion filtration into the \(\ell\)-torsion.
Applying \eqref{eq:local-odd-k} gives
\[
  \log\ell\, V^-(E,\ell,j)
       \ll \ell n_E^2(j+1)\log^2(j\ell F).
\]
For each fixed odd divisor \(\ell\) the sum over \(j\) is at most
\(O_\psi(n_E^2t^2\log^2(t\ell F))\). The number of such primes is
fixed, so the total is polynomial in \(\mathcal Nt\).
Finiteness holds in each of the two negative-component
cases.
\label{cor:ordinary-odd-primes-13}

\proofstep{Assembly on the common scale.}

The negative sum can now be added to the positive and dyadic sums on the very same interval. Their common twist cutoff is what permits a single bound for arbitrary finite prime sets.


At this point the bounds have the following scale dependence.
On the common tower window the prime-two contribution
is at most \(\log 2\,B\), where \(B=\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\).
The positive and negative bounds have total size
\(O_\psi(A^C+B)\) for a fixed exponent \(C\), including
the fixed reference-layer depths. By the prime selection,
\[
 \log(qd)\le Y_0^{\varepsilon/4}
\]
for sufficiently large
\(Y_0\), and \(x\ge Y_0\) on the window.
The factors from \(2^{e(x)+2},N,t\) have logarithm \(O(\log x)\).

Combining the prime-selection term with these polynomial factors yields, uniformly on this window,
\[
 \log A\le Y_0^{\varepsilon/4}+O(\log x)
 \le x^{\varepsilon/4}+O(\log x)\le2x^{\varepsilon/4}
\]
once \(Y_0\) exceeds the fixed threshold. For
each fixed exponent \(C\), the two exponential scales to absorb are
\[
         A^C\le \exp(2Cx^{\varepsilon/4}),\qquad
         B=\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5}).
\]
Their exponents are \(o(x^\varepsilon)\), so the three prime
contributions fit in \(\exp(x^\varepsilon/2)\) eventually. This proves the
assembly of \eqref{eq:cohomwin} from the stated cohomology
and automorphic inputs on the chosen auxiliary primes.
\end{proof}
